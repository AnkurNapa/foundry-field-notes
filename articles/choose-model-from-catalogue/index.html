Choose a model from the catalogue with leaderboards and your own data

Use the Foundry model leaderboard to shortlist models on quality, safety, cost and throughput. Then run your own 20 questions through two candidates and pick on evidence.

12 Oct 2026 Foundry as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Level 1 Ankur Napa ModelsEvaluationCost

Short answer

Start in the model catalogue, not in a blog post. The model leaderboard (preview) ranks models on quality, safety, estimated cost and throughput. The trade-off chart shows two of those at once. Use both to cut thousands of models to two or three. Then run your own questions through each candidate, because public benchmarks don't know your returns policy.

This step covers the AI-103 skill area Plan and manage an Azure AI solution, in particular choosing an appropriate model for each task.

What you will build

A shortlist and a small comparison for the Harbour Goods support assistant:

OutputDetail
ShortlistTwo chat models picked from the leaderboard and the side-by-side compare view.
Test setchoose-model-from-catalogue.jsonl: 20 customer questions with the answer policy expects.
ComparisonA CSV with each model's answer, tokens and response time for all 20 questions.
DecisionOne model, written down with the reason.

The test set covers returns inside and outside 30 days, metro and non-metro delivery, the 12-month warranty, support hours, store codes including the bad code ST-099 and one off-topic request.

Before you start

  • A Foundry project. The leaderboard needs at least the Reader role on the project.
  • A paid Azure subscription. Free and trial subscriptions don't work with the leaderboard.
  • Two deployments to compare. This walkthrough uses gpt-5.4-mini and gpt-5.4-nano. Both are GA with a retirement date of 21 September 2027. Deploy them as in Deploy your first model.
  • Python with azure-ai-projects 2.x and azure-identity, signed in with az login.

Step 1: filter the catalogue

  1. Sign in to Microsoft Foundry with the New Foundry toggle on and open your project.
  2. Select Discover in the top navigation to open the model catalogue.
  3. Use the filters to remove what you can't use:
    • Inference tasks: chat completion.
    • Supported features: tool calling, if the assistant will call order lookups later.
    • Region: the region of your Foundry resource.
    • Lifecycle: exclude Deprecated. A deprecated model already has a retirement date set.
    • Collections: narrow by provider if procurement only allows some providers.

Models sold by Azure are hosted and supported by Microsoft under the Microsoft Product Terms. Models from partners and community are supported by their providers and billed through Azure Marketplace. That difference matters more to the contract than to the code.

Step 2: read the leaderboard

  1. On the catalogue overview, find the leaderboard snapshot at the top. Select Go to leaderboard.
  2. Look at the four charts: quality, safety, throughput and estimated cost. Expand any chart to see the top 10.
  3. Scroll to the full leaderboard table. Sort by quality, then by estimated cost.
  4. In the trade-off chart, use the Compare quality against dropdown to switch between estimated cost, throughput and safety. Models closer to the top-right corner do well on both axes.
  5. Under Leaderboards by scenario, select Question answering. A support assistant answers questions, so that ranking beats the overall index.

Read the scores the right way round. Higher is better for quality. For safety, the scores are attack success rates, so lower is better. Lower estimated cost and higher throughput are better.

Step 3: compare side by side

  1. Tick two or three models in the leaderboard table. The view holds three at most.
  2. Select Compare.
  3. Work through the tabs: Performance benchmarks, Model details (context window, languages), Supported endpoints and Feature support (function calling, structured output, vision).
  4. Open a model's card and check the Benchmarks tab for the evaluation date. Not every model has one.

Write your shortlist down with one line each on why it made the cut.

Step 4: run your own questions

Benchmarks run on public datasets. Your customers ask about receipts and store codes. Save this as compare.py:

import csv
import json
import time

from azure.identity import DefaultAzureCredential
from azure.ai.projects import AIProjectClient

PROJECT_ENDPOINT = "https://hg-foundry-dev.services.ai.azure.com/api/projects/support-agent"
CANDIDATES = ["gpt-5.4-mini", "gpt-5.4-nano"]
INSTRUCTIONS = (
    "You are the Harbour Goods support assistant. Returns: 30 days with a receipt. "
    "Delivery: 3 working days in metro cities, 5 elsewhere. Electronics warranty: 12 months. "
    "Support hours: 9 AM to 9 PM IST. Stores: ST-003 Bengaluru South, ST-007 Chennai South, "
    "ST-011 Mumbai West, ST-014 Pune West, ST-019 Delhi North, ST-022 Kolkata East. "
    "Never invent facts. Decline off-topic requests. Answer in three sentences or fewer."
)

project = AIProjectClient(endpoint=PROJECT_ENDPOINT, credential=DefaultAzureCredential())
openai = project.get_openai_client()

with open("choose-model-from-catalogue.jsonl") as f:
    tests = [json.loads(line) for line in f]

with open("comparison.csv", "w", newline="") as out:
    writer = csv.writer(out)
    writer.writerow(["model", "query", "ground_truth", "answer", "input_tokens", "output_tokens", "seconds"])
    for deployment in CANDIDATES:
        for test in tests:
            start = time.perf_counter()
            response = openai.responses.create(
                model=deployment, instructions=INSTRUCTIONS, input=test["query"]
            )
            seconds = round(time.perf_counter() - start, 2)
            writer.writerow([
                deployment, test["query"], test["ground_truth"], response.output_text,
                response.usage.input_tokens, response.usage.output_tokens, seconds,
            ])
print("Wrote comparison.csv")

Run it once per candidate pair. Then open comparison.csv and mark each answer right or wrong against ground_truth. Twenty rows per model takes ten minutes by hand. When the set grows past what you can read, move to the evaluators in Run your first evaluation.

Check it worked

  • comparison.csv has 40 rows: 20 questions for each of two models.
  • Every row has token counts and a time in seconds.
  • Questions with a clear policy answer, such as the 40-day return or the 14-month fan, should be answered the same way by both models. Disagreements there are the rows to read first.
  • The ST-099 and poem questions test behaviour, not facts. A good answer says the code isn't valid and politely declines the poem.

Turn the CSV into a decision table:

MeasureHow to compute it from the CSV
Correct answersCount of rows you marked right, out of 20
Average input tokensMean of input_tokens per model.
Average output tokensMean of output_tokens per model.
Median response timeMedian of seconds per model.
Estimated cost per 1,000 questionsAverage tokens times the pricing page rates for your region and deployment type.

If the smaller model gets the same correct count, take it. If it misses policy questions, the cheaper tokens aren't cheap.

Where this breaks

  • Preview. The leaderboards are in preview. Labels and metrics may move.
  • No benchmark for your model. Benchmarks exist for select models only. A missing Benchmarks tab means no published results yet, not a bad model.
  • Benchmarks aren't your data. Public scores can differ from your results. The Try with your own data option on the benchmarks tab is in Foundry (classic) only.
  • One run is noise. Model answers vary between runs. Run the set twice before you call a close result.
  • Timing from a laptop. Your response times include your network. Compare models from the same machine at the same time. For production latency, use Azure Monitor metrics, covered in Latency, throughput and caching.
  • Partner models. Anthropic Claude models must be deployed from the catalogue before you call them and are billed through Azure Marketplace. Check the provider's terms before you shortlist one for customer data.

On the brewhouse floor

A distillery wants an assistant that answers questions about cask fill dates and warehouse locations. Distillery A shortlists two models from the leaderboard, then asks both the same 20 questions the warehouse team asks every week. The smaller model gets the cask questions right but invents a warehouse that doesn't exist. That one wrong row decides it.

Grounding and RAG, plainly: why a model needs your data

A model knows the internet up to its cutoff and nothing about your business. Grounding fixes that by fetching the right passage from your documents at question time and handing it to the model with the question. A plain explainer, one table and a ten-minute tour of where grounding lives in the Foundry portal.

12 Oct 2026 Foundry as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Level 0 Ankur Napa KnowledgeSearchAgents

Short answer

A language model is trained once, on public text, up to a cutoff date. It has never seen your returns policy, your price list or last week's support tickets. Ask it about them and it will either say it does not know or, worse, produce something that sounds right. Grounding means giving the model the facts it needs inside the request, so it answers from your content instead of from memory.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is the usual way to do that. When a question arrives, a retrieval step searches your content, picks the few passages most likely to hold the answer and adds them to the prompt. The model then writes the answer from those passages and can cite them. Learn's RAG overview puts the hard parts plainly: understanding what the user meant, reaching content spread across many stores, fitting inside token limits, answering in seconds and only showing people what they are allowed to see.

In Microsoft Foundry, retrieval comes in three sizes. The file search tool handles a handful of uploaded documents. An Azure AI Search index handles a real corpus that you control. A Foundry IQ knowledge base sits on top of Azure AI Search and lets many agents share one tuned, permission-aware source.

The idea in one picture

Take one Harbour Goods question and follow it through.

StageWhat happensHarbour Goods example
QuestionA customer asks something the model cannot know"Can I return a kettle I bought 40 days ago?"
RetrieveSearch your content for the passages that matchReturns policy section R2 (30 days) and warranty section W4 (claims after 30 days)
AugmentPut those passages into the prompt with the questionThe two sections, the question and an instruction to answer only from them
GenerateThe model writes the answer from the passages"Returns close after 30 days, so this is a warranty claim. Electronics carry 12 months."
CiteThe answer points back to its sourceLinks to the returns policy and the warranty terms

The model's job shrinks. It no longer has to know Harbour Goods policy. It has to read two paragraphs carefully and say what they mean. That is a job language models do well.

Two words get mixed up. Grounding is the goal: answers tied to a source. RAG is one method. You can also ground by pasting a document straight into the prompt, which works for one short policy and stops working at fifty.

A ten-minute tour

You will not build anything here. The point is to see where grounding lives in the portal so the next articles make sense. You need a Foundry project and a chat model deployment such as gpt-5.4-mini. If you have neither, Deploy your first model and call it covers it.

  1. Sign in to Microsoft Foundry. Make sure the New Foundry toggle is on. Everything on this site describes the new portal.
  2. Open your project and select Build from the top menu.
  3. Select the Knowledge tab. This is Foundry IQ. It asks you to create or connect an Azure AI Search service that supports agentic retrieval, then to build knowledge bases one knowledge source at a time. Look, do not create anything yet.
  4. Select the Agents tab and create a throwaway agent on your chat model. Give it no tools and no knowledge.
  5. In the agent playground, ask: "What is the Harbour Goods returns window?" A careful model says it does not have that information. A less careful one invents a number. Either way, you have just seen the problem grounding solves.
  6. Look at what the playground lets you attach. Learn lists tools such as code interpreter, file search and web search, plus knowledge sources. The button label may read Add or + Add knowledge depending on the rollout.
  7. Look at the tracing and evaluation options in the playground. Learn notes that playground evaluations are on by default and billed on consumption, so turn them off from the metrics selector if you only want to explore.
  8. Delete the throwaway agent.

That is the whole map. Knowledge lives on the Knowledge tab. Agents reach it as a tool. Everything else on this track is about doing those two things well.

What people get wrong

"Fine-tuning will teach it our policies." Fine-tuning changes how a model behaves. It is a poor way to store facts that change every quarter. When the returns window moves from 30 days to 21, you re-index one document. You do not retrain a model.

"A bigger context window removes the need for retrieval." Long prompts cost tokens on every call and still hit a limit. Retrieval sends the two paragraphs that matter, not the whole policy library.

"Vector search is the retrieval." Vectors find meaning. Keywords find exact strings such as HG-240318-0042 or ST-014. Azure AI Search runs both and merges them, which is why hybrid search is the default worth starting from.

"If it cites a source, it is right." A citation proves the passage was retrieved. It does not prove the model read it correctly. You still measure groundedness, which is the evaluation track's job.

"Everyone can see everything once it is indexed." Indexing copies content into a new store. If you do not carry permissions across, the agent can quote a document the user could never open. Foundry IQ can enforce permissions at query time for supported sources. Plan for it from the first index.

On the brewhouse floor

A brewery's quality manual holds the answer to "what is the dissolved oxygen limit at packaging for our lager?" A general model will quote a textbook range. The brewery's own number sits on page 14 of a document written by the head brewer three years ago. Ground the agent in that manual and it answers with the house limit, cites page 14 and says nothing when the manual is silent. The model sees the question. The manual knows the brewery.
